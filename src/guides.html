How to build a fair seven-day CWL roster

The problem. “Fair” can mean equal days, strongest lineup or keeping every account involved. Those goals conflict unless the clan names its priority before assigning players.

Workflow

  1. Separate non-negotiable Core players from Rotation candidates and true Reserves.
  2. Collect availability for all seven days before deciding appearance counts.
  3. Fill every day's required team size first. Only then compare appearance totals.
  4. For similar-strength Rotation players, give the next slot to the player with fewer planned days unless a difficult opponent justifies a stronger lineup.
  5. Publish the plan and the exception rule before Day 1.

Mistakes and trade-offs

Do not promise equal days before checking coverage, count an unavailable day as being benched, or use a hidden strength rule. A stronger opponent may justify fewer rotations, but name that exception and revisit later rounds.

Guide 02 · 4 August 2026 · ClashPanel

Rotate players without weakening every war day

The problem. Changing several players every day may look fair but removes lineup stability and can make all seven rounds weaker.

Workflow

  1. Protect a Core group that is large enough to anchor every complete lineup.
  2. Create a smaller Rotation group with overlapping availability.
  3. Change players in blocks rather than replacing the whole edge of the roster daily.
  4. Before a difficult matchup, select the strongest available rotation; use lower-risk rounds to rebalance appearances.
  5. Keep at least one Reserve uncommitted where availability is uncertain.

Original example. In a sample 15v15 clan with 13 Core and four Rotation players, only two rotation spots are needed per day. Keeping the same pair for Days 1–2 and changing one position on Day 3 creates fewer handoffs than using a new pair every day, while still opening appearances over the week.

Mistakes and limits

Do not rotate someone into a day they marked unavailable, treat Reserve as a punishment, or assume opponent difficulty is known exactly. Stability helps coordination, but it should not trap one player on the bench for the entire week.

Guide 03 · 4 August 2026 · ClashPanel

Plan CWL when availability changes

The problem. A seven-day plan becomes unreliable when an account changes availability after lineups were shared.

Workflow

  1. Record availability per account, not only per person; one member may control several accounts with different commitments.
  2. Mark confirmed “no” separately from unknown. Unknown is a risk, not an available player.
  3. After a change, inspect only affected days first and count the remaining active lineup.
  4. Promote an already assigned Reserve before moving a player between clans.
  5. If a move is necessary, verify the source clan remains complete on every day.
  6. Tell affected players what changed and why.

Trade-off. Late changes can make equal appearances impossible. Prioritise a complete legal lineup, then document how the missed opportunity will be handled in a later round or season.

Guide 04 · 4 August 2026 · ClashPanel

Use attack and defence performance together

The problem. Ranking only by stars ignores defensive results and attack difficulty; combining everything into one unexplained score hides the opposite problem.

Workflow

  1. Check attack count before comparing averages.
  2. Separate raw stars from difficulty-adjusted context.
  3. Review conceded stars and destruction only when defensive details exist.
  4. Look for repeated patterns across rounds or seasons, not one bad attack.
  5. Keep the two sides visible when discussing roster changes.

Original example. Sample A averages 2.4 adjusted stars and concedes 1.8. Sample B averages 2.6 but has no complete defensive data. The honest conclusion is that Sample B had the stronger measured offense; it is not that Sample B was the stronger overall player.

Mistakes and limitations

Do not turn missing defence into zero conceded stars, ignore cleanup context or treat a Town Hall mismatch as a complete measure of difficulty. Base design and opponent choice remain outside the metric.

Guide 05 · 4 August 2026 · ClashPanel

Handle missed attacks fairly

The problem. A missed-attack count can identify a coordination issue, but it does not explain why the attack was missed.

Workflow

  1. Confirm the war has complete member details and the player was actually in that day's roster.
  2. Wait for the API to reflect the final war state before publishing a count.
  3. Separate a technical or emergency exception from an unexplained miss.
  4. Apply the clan's stated rule consistently.
  5. Use repeated misses as a planning signal; do not publicly shame someone from one number.
Sample policy. “A completed war with one unused attack triggers a private check-in. Repeated unexplained misses affect Core status; confirmed emergencies do not.” This is a transparent policy example, not a requirement built into ClashPanel.

Mistakes and limits

Do not count unknown details as a miss, compare someone who played two rounds with someone who played seven without context, or use the tracker as the final disciplinary decision.

Guide 06 · 4 August 2026 · ClashPanel

Choose CWL bonus recipients transparently

The problem. Bonus medals are limited, and a hidden or changing selection rule damages trust even when the recipients played well.

Workflow

  1. State whether the clan prioritises contribution, development, reliability or a mixed approach before the season ends.
  2. Review incremental star contribution, attack difficulty, missed attacks and available performance context.
  3. Check whether cleanup attacks duplicate an earlier result or add new stars/destruction.
  4. Document manual exceptions such as leadership work or agreed rotation sacrifice.
  5. Publish the reason, not only the names.

Original example. Sample A earns the first two stars on a base. Sample B later repeats two stars with five more percent destruction. A raw-star table calls them equal; an incremental-contribution view recognises that the first attack changed the war score more. A clan may still reward Sample B for another stated reason.

Mistakes and limits

Do not claim the recommendation is objective truth, hide the selected strategy or infer off-platform contributions. The software supports an explanation; leadership owns the decision.

Guide 07 · 4 August 2026 · ClashPanel

Analyse several CWL seasons instead of one week

The problem. One week mixes roster changes, matchup luck and small samples. A multi-season view adds context but can also compare different conditions unfairly.

Workflow

  1. Check data quality and war-detail completeness for every season.
  2. Compare league, finish, record, attack usage, star differential and destruction differential.
  3. Note roster or Town Hall changes before attributing a trend to performance.
  4. Separate recorded league values from defensibly reconstructed movement.
  5. Investigate a repeated direction over several comparable seasons.

Trade-off.

More seasons increase context but also increase the chance that roster strength, game balance or provider coverage changed. Keep the comparison descriptive.

Guide 08 · 4 August 2026 · ClashPanel

Spreadsheet planning versus a connected CWL planner

The problem. A spreadsheet is flexible and shareable, but tags, availability and live results often have to be copied between tabs or tools.

Use a spreadsheet when

  • the clan needs a one-off custom model;
  • the organiser must work offline;
  • the process depends on private columns that should never enter another service;
  • the team already has a stable review workflow and does not need live API context.

Use a connected planner when

  • player and clan tags should load current public game context;
  • one player pool feeds several clans;
  • availability polls should connect to assignments;
  • the final plan should feed a live Operation Board;
  • the organiser wants warnings, locks and reversible suggestions instead of maintaining formulas.

Practical workflow. Keep the spreadsheet as the import or offline source, import only the tag columns needed for the plan, review every resolved player, then export or retain your own backup. ClashPanel does not make the original sheet unnecessary.

Mistakes and limitations

Do not assume an imported name identifies the right account without its tag, include private notes that the planner does not need or discard the original workbook before verifying the plan. Live API access can fail; a spreadsheet remains useful as a fallback.
