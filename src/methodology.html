Product methodology

See how each recommendation is built.

ClashPanel uses explicit rules and available game data. It does not replace a clan leader's judgement, and missing data is shown as missing rather than filled with invented results.

01 / planning

CWL Auto Plan

Problem. A shared player pool has to become complete 15v15 or 30v30 clan rosters without ignoring availability, roles or locked assignments.

Inputs and rules

  • Active clans, CWL league, roster size, player Town Hall, role locks, clan locks and player availability.
  • When performance history is available, the fit score weighs performance, reliability, recent form, Town Hall level, matchup context, CWL-specific scope and confidence. Missing history uses a conservative Town Hall-based fallback and receives a confidence penalty.
  • Higher-league clans are filled first. Unavailable players are excluded; role and assignment locks are preserved. Reserve depth is capped by roster size.
  • Core players are assigned first, Rotation players fill remaining positions and Reserves provide depth where the roster still has a gap.

What the output means

The preview is a starting distribution with clan assignments, role counts, expected stars only when every selected player's value is known and explicit warnings for incomplete rosters or excess reserve depth. Applying it remains a user action.

Limits and overrides

League bands and fit weights are product rules, not proof of future results. Availability can be outdated, opponents can change and historical samples can be small. Leaders should override roles for leadership duties, known account sharing, strategic specialist use or information that is not present in ClashPanel.

02 / planning

Optimise Plan suggestions

Problem. A nearly finished plan may still have an incomplete clan, too much reserve depth or an uneven role distribution. Rebuilding the whole plan would hide which decisions changed.

The optimiser works sequentially. It first tries to fill required clan rosters, then removes excess depth, then proposes refinements. It can promote a local Reserve, move an eligible unlocked player from a sufficiently covered clan, or suggest a smaller role change. Started clans, role locks and assignment locks are protected, and a suggestion is rejected if it breaks required coverage elsewhere.

Suggestions are shown individually with reasons and actions. They are not silently applied. A leader should reject a technically valid move when it conflicts with real availability, clan membership expectations or a deliberate development roster.

Worked sample. Sample Clan B has 14 active players for a 15v15 roster while Sample Clan A has 17 and remains complete after one unlocked Rotation player moves. The optimiser may suggest that move. If Clan A would fall below required coverage, the suggestion is not produced.

03 / context

Player performance context

Problem. Raw stars alone do not show whether attacks were reliable, recent or made into harder bases.

Where provider data is available, ClashPanel keeps performance, reliability, attack count, average stars, matchup scope, confidence and recent-form delta separate. The Auto Plan fit score combines those signals but also exposes low-confidence or unavailable history. It does not claim machine learning and it does not turn a small sample into certainty.

A value marked unavailable means the provider or required history did not supply enough usable information. The fallback helps ordering continue, but it must not be presented as measured player history. Leaders should compare the sample size and confidence before using a performance difference to change a roster.

04 / review

Attack versus defence comparison

Problem. A player can attack well and still have difficult defensive matchups, or concede few stars while taking easier offensive targets.

For current CWL data, offensive stars can be adjusted by a matchup-strength multiplier based on Town Hall and available progression context. Defence is kept separate: average conceded stars and destruction are converted into a bounded defensive rating where fewer stars and less destruction are better. Historical pages show offensive averages, triple rate and low-star rate beside defensive averages when complete war details exist.

No single combined number proves player quality. Base design, opponent selection, cleanup attacks and incomplete member details can change the interpretation. ClashPanel therefore shows missing defence as unavailable instead of zero.

05 / seasons

CWL history and league movement

Problem. One war day is noisy. Leaders need season totals and multi-season direction without pretending every older source is complete.

Completed wars feed wins, losses, draws, average stars, destruction, attack usage, close-war count and roster summaries. League and finishing position use recorded provider values where available. For legacy history, reconstruction only fills a league path when the known movement rules and played seasons support it; ambiguous values remain unknown.

Historical data quality and war-detail completeness are visible. League movement describes a change in recorded or defensibly reconstructed league, not the cause of that change. Provider outages, missing wars and roster changes limit cross-season comparisons.

06 / participation

Missed-attack tracking

Problem. Counting a missing attack incorrectly can unfairly penalise a player when the war details or available-attack count are incomplete.

For completed wars with complete details, available attacks equal team size multiplied by attacks per member. Clan-level missed attacks are the non-negative difference between available and used attacks. Player-level missed attacks compare that player's available attacks with actual attacks for each round played.

If any required war detail is incomplete, reliability is marked unknown and the missed value becomes unavailable rather than an assumed zero. Leaders should still confirm late API updates, emergency substitutions and agreed exceptions before treating the count as a conduct issue.

07 / recognition

Bonus medal recommendations

Problem. Raw total stars can reward cleanup duplication or ignore difficulty, reliability and contribution timing.

The Operation Board derives contribution from ordered attacks, improvement over the best previous result on a base, destruction improvement, matchup difficulty and available performance context. Strategy controls can change how factors are weighted, and the visible recommendation remains advice rather than an automatic allocation.

Missing attack order or incomplete war details reduce what can be evaluated. Leadership, donations, substitutions and other off-platform contributions are not measured. The final recipients should be reviewed and explained by clan leadership.

Worked sample. If Sample A earns the first two stars on a base and Sample B later repeats two stars with slightly more destruction, their raw stars are equal but their incremental contribution is not. ClashPanel keeps that distinction visible; a leader can still choose a different policy and explain it.

08 / boundaries

Shared data and API limitations

  • ClashPanel depends on submitted tags, authenticated plan data, the official Clash of Clans API and selected history providers. Those sources can be delayed, incomplete or temporarily unavailable.
  • Unknown data is not proof of zero activity. A fallback used for planning is not labelled as measured history.
  • Predictions and ratings are deterministic product estimates. They are not guaranteed outcomes, verified accuracy claims or machine-learning results.
  • Demo values on public pages are clearly labelled sample data and do not identify a live clan or user.
  • Clan leaders remain responsible for checking output against current availability, clan rules and information the software cannot observe.
